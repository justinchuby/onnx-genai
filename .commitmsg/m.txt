perf(speculative): a rejected proposal no longer copies the whole KV cache

First residency fix from the CUDA plan, taking its ordering: rollback before
`propose_chained`, because it is both the largest transfer and the smallest
change.

Rolling back a rejected proposal called `host_copy_of` on every
`rollback_state` cell — the entire KV cache, device to host, to drop a few
positions — then uploaded it again on the next bind. Per rejection. The report
identifies it as the single worst copy in the workflow, and it is: the other
sites move a logits row or a carry, this one moves the cache.

`truncate_state_cell` avoids it where the shape allows. A rollback keeps a
*prefix* along the sequence axis; when every axis before that one has extent 1 —
a batch-1 seq-major cache, which is what the native backend declares — the kept
elements are already a contiguous prefix of the same buffer. The truncation is
then a smaller view of it: `alias_with_shape`, no copy, and a device tensor
stays on the device.

It returns `None` rather than guessing when the kept elements are strided. A
head-major `[batch, heads, sequence, head_dim]` cache interleaves its sequence
across heads, so a prefix of the axis is not a prefix of the buffer; returning a
view there would hand back the wrong elements, which is worse than the copy it
would have avoided. Those cells fall back to the host path, and the fallback now
says at its call site that this is where the large transfer happens rather than
leaving it implicit inside `host_copy_of`. A strided device truncate needs an
execution-provider copy and is the remaining work.

Five tests, including the one that makes the fast path safe at all: the view and
the host truncation must agree element for element across every length. Two
implementations of one operation are only trustworthy as a pair.
